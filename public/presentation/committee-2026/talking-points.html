Opening · 3 min

Slide 1live homepage

What this is

  • Let the dots animate for a few seconds before speaking.
  • “This is the site as it is today. 10,021 Jewish-owned businesses in Berlin, placed on the streets where they stood.”
  • Say the one sentence from the band. That is the frame for everything that follows.
  • You funded this a year ago on paper. Today you see it.
Slide 2the foundation

Where the data comes from

  • Source: Christoph Kreutzmüller, Ausverkauf (2012), and the HU Berlin database. The 16 narrated stories: Christine Fischer-Defoy and the Aktives Museum.
  • Checked in 2026: 9,177 of 10,021 records match the source on every field. The rest were 861 field differences, 607 fixed automatically, 254 flagged for manual review.
  • Three languages: German, English, Yiddish.
  • Don’t skip this slide. It is for the sceptics in the room.
Slide 3what the archive holds

The numbers, honestly

  • 10,021 documented. 8,838 placed on the map. 9,679 closed between 1933 and 1945.
  • Name the gap before anyone asks: 1,183 have no resolvable historical address. We don’t guess.
  • Read the quote: behind every dot a family, a livelihood, a place where Berlin bought its bread.

Time as a slider

  • Click into the map. Drag the year from 1933 to 1938. Point out: mint turns yellow, then orange.
  • Click one cluster, open one business, read the name and the dates.

What we are building · 8 min

Slide 6archive, street, museum

Memory in three places

  • Archive: online. Street: plaques, in progress. Museum: prototype.
  • They reinforce each other: the plaque leads to the map, the map to the museum, the museum back to the street.
  • Beyond Berlin: Frankfurt pilot, 167 businesses. The method transfers to any city with a source like Berlin’s.
Slides 7–8E. Braun & Co.

One name

  • Slide 7, 30 seconds: Viennese luxury store next to the Hotel Adlon, Unter den Linden. Customers included Käthe Dorsch and Paul Hindemith.
  • Slide 8: 1914 opens · 1933 owners spared as Austrian nationals · 1938 arrested, forced sale · 1943 closed, seized, bombed · 1954 sale declared void. The Adlon covers the site today.
  • Every dot has a story like this. For 16 we have told it; for 10,000 we know name, trade, address, dates.
  • If asked about the plaque saying Nr. 75: that is the historical number; the block was renumbered.
Slide 9plaques

Design, proof, prototype

  • Two formats: 300 × 200 mm with story and storefront engraving; 300 × 100 mm compact with the four facts.
  • 18 plaques with engraving files. 181 addresses on the contact list for owners and districts.
  • Every plaque names four facts (name, trade, dates, place) and carries the context line: expropriated and liquidated under National Socialist rule. Stiftung Zurückgeben on every one.
  • Status, in these words: designed, verified, prototypes made. No plaque is installed yet. Final fabrication waits on sites.
Slide 10exhibit

The kiosk already runs

  • The plan: walk-on floor map, three scales (case, room, hall) because we don’t know which venue says yes.
  • The kiosk is not a rendering. Touch interface with attract loop and automatic reset runs in a browser today. It needs a room.
  • If asked to see it: K jumps to Appendix 1, the live kiosk.
Slide 11classrooms

Free teaching material, grades 8 to 13

  • “Six lessons from one archive”: six single-lesson units with worksheets. “The street you walk on”: a three-phase neighbourhood walk workbook.
  • Three languages, no login, no cost. What is missing: a school that tries it and tells us what fails.

Slide 13next twelve months

Four steps and what each needs

  • Q4: first plaques go up, three to five pilot addresses. Needs owners, district office, a workshop, a small installation budget.
  • Q1: landing pages for museums and for teachers. Needs two conversations with venues, two with schools.
  • Q2: kiosk in a partner venue. Needs a venue, a socket, a screen.
  • Q3: Frankfurt complete, one more city begun. Needs a source and a local research partner.
  • Stay honest: the landing pages are planned, not built. The kiosk exists, the venue doesn’t.

The ask · 10 min or more

Slide 16where we need you

Not money. Ideas and doors.

  • Open the cards one at a time with keys 1 to 4. Read the yellow line on each aloud, then stop talking. Tolerate the silence.
  • 1 Museums and memorial sites: a curator who will try the kiosk for 30 minutes. Jewish Museum, Topography of Terror, district museums, Stolpersteine.
  • 2 Schools and teachers: a history teacher who will say honestly what fails in the classroom. Landesinstitut, subject associations.
  • 3 Plaques in the city: one property owner on Unter den Linden or Friedrichstraße who says yes. Which district office is known to be open?
  • 4 Press, research, funding programmes: a programme officer who tells us which application not to write. Which desk would show the map, not just mention it?
  • Write every name down as it is said. Close with: one introduction per person is enough; we follow up within 48 hours.
