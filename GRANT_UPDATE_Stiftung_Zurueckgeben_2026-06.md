I am pleased to share where the StoryTimeMaps project stands at the midpoint of this grant. The project set out to map formerly Jewish-owned businesses across Berlin using verified historical records and archival imagery, and then to carry that memory into the physical city through memorial plaques and a public museum exhibit. The digital foundation of that work is now substantially complete and publicly live, and the physical and exhibit phases are underway.

## What has been accomplished

**A complete, public, interactive map is live at storytimemaps.com.** Visitors can explore **10,021 documented Jewish-owned Berlin businesses** on an interactive map, move through a 1920–1945 timeline that shows enterprises operating and then facing forced closure and "Aryanization," search and filter the full directory, and open individual business stories with archival photographs and historical context. The site is built to be read by Berliners, descendants, students, and international visitors alike: it is **multilingual from the ground up — English, German, and Yiddish.**

**The data is sourced and verified, not approximate.** Every one of the 10,021 records is drawn from the *Datenbank jüdischer Gewerbebetriebe in Berlin* at the Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin, the scholarly database grounded in Dr. Christoph Kreutzmüller's research on the destruction of Jewish commercial life, with archival material informed by the Leo Baeck Institute. In May–June 2026 we completed a **full re-verification of all 10,021 records against the original source**: 91.6% matched on every checked field, we applied 607 conservative corrections (restoring truncated street names and diacritics), flagged 254 records for careful editorial review, and improved 251 map coordinates through street-confirmed geocoding. Every record now carries its **source link and a verification date.** Roughly 90% of businesses are plotted at specific street addresses; the remainder sit at neighbourhood level pending finer geocoding.

**The groundwork for the physical phases already exists:**

- **15 memorial plaques** have been fully designed, each in two styles (a Berlin *Gedenktafel*-inspired design and an engraved brass style), each carrying a QR code that links the street to the business's full online story.
- A **museum-exhibit prototype is built and running** — a touch-screen "exhibit mode" of the map, designed for kiosk display, with an attract loop and visitor sessions. A public "exhibit vision" page presents the concept to prospective museum and cultural partners.
- A **building-owner outreach system** is live: any visitor at a historic address can submit an inquiry to host a plaque, and we maintain a research file on current occupants and property types for an initial set of priority sites.
- Deeper **narrative research** has been completed for ~57 businesses (with archival imagery gathered for many), the basis for the first wave of plaques and exhibit stories.

The technical platform itself is production-grade: Next.js, Mapbox GL, a Supabase backend (supporting user accounts and community-contributed corrections), eight visual themes, and a documented, reproducible data pipeline. A small **Frankfurt pilot (57 businesses)** demonstrates that the model extends to other German cities.
